## The post

Most businesses have one process that quietly eats a day a week, and everyone has stopped noticing it.

Reading a 60-page approval and typing the conditions into a spreadsheet.
Checking a set of plans against the code, clause by clause, by hand.
Rebuilding the same quote from the same rate sheets, again.
Pulling numbers out of one PDF so someone can put them into another PDF.

Nobody budgets for these. They just happen, every week, forever.

They don't get fixed because fixing them has always meant a three-month project, a discovery phase, and a quote with a two in front of it. So it never clears the bar, and everyone goes back to doing it by hand.

That's the part that has changed.

I've spent two years building the boring half — sign-in, permissions, document handling, compliance, billing — as reusable parts. Fifty-odd of them, already running in production across other systems. So when I take on a job, none of that gets built again. I build the thing you actually asked for.

Which is why it's three weeks instead of three months.

What that looks like:

→ **Week 1.** I sit with whoever actually does the work, not just whoever signs, and we write down what "done" means.
→ **Week 2.** It's on a private link by about day eight, and you're using it while it's still being built.
→ **Week 3.** Into your environment, on your accounts, your team trained on it. You own it outright — no licence, no lock-in.

Fixed scope, fixed price. If it runs over, that's mine, not yours.

And if you'd rather see it work before committing to it, that's what the one-week audit is for. I map where the hours actually go, rank the three best targets, and build one of them so you can click it. $2,500 + GST, credited in full against the build if you go ahead.

If there's a process in your business that eats hours and shouldn't, I'd genuinely like to hear what it is.

  it is warmer than anything cold.
