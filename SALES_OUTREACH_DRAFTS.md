## The rules, which matter more than the wording

1. **Send from your own account, one at a time.** Not a sequencer, not a merge field that shows.
   40 names is a morning of typing, not a campaign.
2. **One ask per message, and make it small.** "Is there a process that eats time?" gets answered.
   "Would you like to discuss AI transformation?" does not.
3. **Never attach a deck.** The link to `/services` is the deck.
4. **The question goes last** and it should be answerable in one line. Every message below ends on
   one; do not add a paragraph after it.
5. **Personalise the first sentence or don't send it.** One clause proving you know who they are
   beats three paragraphs of positioning. If you can't write that clause, they aren't warm and they
   belong in a later cohort.
6. **Follow up once.** §5 is the most valuable message here and the one most likely to be skipped.

## 4b. Business brokers → Kira Exec ⭐ *(the 11 connected 31 Jul – 5 Aug)*

**This is a different product to everything else in this file.** Brokers do not buy builds — they
hold the book of exiting owners Kira Exec needs, and they earn commission on a sale. So the pitch is
not "AI for your business," it is **"your listing is discounted because it runs on the owner's head,
and that is fixable while they're still trading."** Every broker in Australia has listings stuck on
exactly this.

**Their incentive is money, not technology:** an owner-dependent business sells at a discount or does
not sell at all. Fix the dependency, the multiple goes up, their commission goes up. That is the
entire argument and it should be made in their language, not ours.

### 🚨 DO THIS BEFORE ANY FURTHER BROKER MESSAGE — add all 11 as introducers

**Verified in the Kira repo 2026-08-05: every promise in the sent message is genuinely built.**
Dashboard with status + valuation movement (`app/introducer/page.tsx`), content wall enforced
SERVER-side by `introducer_owner_projection()`, `@caistech/attribution` wired through
`attachFirstTouch`, magic-link entry, undertaking acceptance, admin add-and-invite.

**The problem: none of the 11 has an introducer row, so none has a referral token.**

Attribution runs off the token — `introducerByReferralToken()` → `recordClick()` →
`attachFirstTouch()`. **No token in the link means no attribution record at all.** So if a broker
forwards a plain `kiraexec.com` link today and that owner signs up, Kira gains a customer, the broker
earns nothing, and no record exists that they introduced anyone. That is precisely the failure the
sent message promised could not happen ("first-touch attribution, so whoever made the introduction is
credited and it can't be quietly reassigned").

Kira's own admin code already says this out loud:

> *"One step on purpose: an introducer row with no invite sent is a broker who thinks they're set up
> and isn't."*

Right now it is worse than that — they aren't set up **and there is no row**.

**Operator decision 2026-08-05: provision each broker when they respond and supply an email.**
Correct call — the admin form needs an email and a LinkedIn DM does not give you one. Two mechanisms
already in the product make this safe, so there is no need to chase emails:

1. **`/advisors` IS the capture step.** `app/api/advisors/enquiry/route.ts` takes first name, last
   name, email, firm, **ABN**, state, requires them to confirm the condition, writes
   `advisor_enquiries` and **emails the operator** ("so it doesn't sit in a table nobody looks at").
   An interested broker self-serves a better record than a DM would produce. **This is the real
   reason to send Follow-up A** — the link correction is the excuse, the capture is the point.
2. **The unattributed-signup window is already handled.** `app/signup/page.tsx`: *"Only ask how they
   heard about us when we don't already know."* A client arriving with no token is asked how they
   heard, so an early forward is recoverable. Treat it as a **hint, not an attribution**
   (`@caistech/attribution`'s own rule) — reconcile it by hand in `/admin/introducers`, never let a
   self-reported name silently write the protected field.

**So the order is:** Follow-up A (correct link → `/advisors` → they enquire → you get their details) →
add them in `/admin/introducers` → invite + referral link goes out → Follow-up B with **their** link.

### Follow-up B — the thing that actually unlocks a referral

The pitch answered *why should I care* and *what's in it for me*. It did not answer **"what do I
actually send my 65-year-old client on Monday?"** That is real work — composing a message about an AI
product to a sceptical older seller, while disclosing you're paid for it — and it is the single most
likely reason an interested broker goes quiet.

**So write it for them.** Send this a few days after A, or immediately to anyone who engages:

> [Name] — one thing that might make this easier.
>
> The awkward part is knowing what to actually say to a client. So here's something you can forward
> as-is, or butcher however you like:
>
> ---
> *"[First name] — worth a look before we go to market.*
>
> *What hurts a sale like yours usually isn't the numbers, it's that so much of how the business
> actually runs sits with you and has never been written down. Buyers price that as risk and it comes
> off what they'll pay.*
>
> *There's a tool now that gets it out of your head while you keep trading — you talk to it as you
> work rather than sitting down to document the business, and what comes out is a handover document
> a buyer's accountant can read without you in the room.*
>
> *[YOUR REFERRAL LINK — the one in your invite email, not kiraexec.com]*
>
> *Being upfront: they pay me a fee if you sign up. I'd have sent it to you either way."*
> ---
>
> That last line is the disclosure handled, so you don't have to word it yourself.
>
> Use the link from your invite email rather than the plain site address — that's what credits the
> introduction to you.

> And the only thing I'd ask: is there one seller on your books right now where this is the problem?
> You don't need to name them — I just want to know I've built the right thing.

**Why this works:** it removes the labour, it hands them the disclosure sentence (their obligation,
solved), and the ask at the end is answerable in one line without committing them to anything.

### Second message — only once they engage

Two points to hold back until they're interested, because they answer the objections a broker will
actually raise:

1. **The content wall.** As an introducer they see their client's *progress* — status, movement — and
   never the contents. Brokers are acutely aware they are handling confidential material; "I can see
   it's working without seeing their books" removes an objection before it is made. (Built:
   `@caistech/coordination-sdk` gives the `introducer`/`broker` role `view_status` **without**
   `view`.)
2. **It also works pre-listing.** An owner two years from exit is not a listing yet, but they are a
   relationship the broker wants to own early. This gives them a reason to have that conversation
   now — which is worth more to a broker than the referral fee.

### What NOT to say to a broker

- ❌ **Don't lead with AI.** They care about the multiple, not the method.
- ❌ **Don't use "organisational intelligence."** That language belongs to the AOI content series and
  it means nothing to someone selling a plumbing business.
- ❌ **Don't quote $999/mo in the first message.** The price is the owner's problem, not the broker's,
  and naming it early makes it a cost conversation instead of a value one.
- ❌ **Don't pitch `/services`.** A broker will never buy an $18,000 build. Mixing the two wastes the
  segment.
- ❌ **Don't send all 11 on the same day.** Send 3, read the replies, adjust the wording, then send
  the rest. The first three are a test, not a campaign.
