# Stream B — pillars and cuts (the volume lane)

> Stream A (`TEMPLATE.md`) is **evidence-bound**: every driplet needs a real failure, a
> verified mechanism, and a number established rather than estimated. That caps it at maybe
> fifteen to twenty more posts, ever. It is the lane that *convinces*.
>
> Stream B is **thesis-bound**: opinion, frameworks, market reads, teardowns. No
> evidentiary cost, effectively unlimited, and it is the lane that *reaches*. It is where
> volume comes from.
>
> **Locked 2026-08-08.** Voice is the same file for both: `foundation/_portfolio/dennis-voice.md`.

---

## The mechanic (what Hormozi is actually doing)

**It is not 100 original ideas a week.** It is a handful of pillar pieces atomised across
formats and platforms. One long recording becomes clips, quotes, carousels, threads,
shorts. The unit of work is **one insight, many cuts** — he is cutting three or four things
a hundred ways, not writing a hundred things.

That is the whole model, and it transfers exactly:

```
1 PILLAR  (1,200–2,500 words — written once, properly)
    │
    ├── the thesis      one claim, 3 lines, the argument's spine
    ├── the list        its structure as a numbered post
    ├── the contrarian  what everyone believes vs what you've watched happen
    ├── the story       one anecdote from it, standing alone
    ├── the question    the open question, posted bare as a prompt
    └── the receipt     the one number or artifact that proves it
```

**Six to eight pieces per pillar.** Four pillars a month is thirty-odd posts without a
single new idea after the first week of each.

## The pillar

**Length:** 1,200–2,500 words. Long enough that the cuts have something to cut.
**Home:** LinkedIn article, or the newsletter, or a page on the site. Not the feed — the
feed gets the cuts.
**Shape:** a claim, the reasoning, what you've actually watched happen, what follows from
it, and what you are still unsure about.

**Pillars available right now, all already thought through:**

| Pillar | The claim |
|---|---|
| Distributor-first | We sell to operators who already have customers, and clip a small fee per end user. Selling to end users is the mistake almost everyone makes. |
| The factory | One operator, one shared substrate, ~38 products. Why the moat is the substrate and not any product. |
| Boomer exit | A large cohort of owners hold enormous latent value that will dissipate, because the business is not sellable while it runs on them. |
| Agent trustworthiness | Broken software looks broken; a broken agent looks fluent. What that changes about how you buy, sell and verify one. |
| Build-to-validate | Build the cheapest demonstrable artifact BEFORE validating, because building sharpens the offer in ways ideation cannot. |
| Phase 1 / Phase 2 | Most AI rollouts stop at setup. Phase 2 is where the system absorbs the knowledge that lives in people's heads — and almost nobody has started. |

## The cuts

Each is a standalone feed post. None needs the pillar to make sense; each should make
someone want it.

**1 · The thesis.** Three lines. The claim, the reason, the consequence. No story.

**2 · The list.** The pillar's structure as a numbered post — five to eight items, one line
each. Highest-shared format, lowest-effort cut.

**3 · The contrarian.** *"Everyone says X. What I've watched happen is Y."* Only where you
genuinely disagree with the room — a manufactured contrarian read is transparent and it
costs the credibility stream A is buying.

**4 · The story.** One anecdote, told properly, ending in what it taught. The closest cut
to a driplet, and the one to reach for when a pillar has a real incident in it.

**5 · The question.** The pillar's open question, posted bare with two lines of setup. Best
comment generator of the six.

**6 · The receipt.** One number, one artifact, one screenshot, one line of explanation. Only
where the number is real (see below).

## What carries over from Stream A, unchanged

- **The voice.** Same file. Self-deprecating, human-first, question-led, no vendor words.
- **Sanitisation.** No client, partner or non-owned product name. Kira may be named.
- **No hashtags. No unicode bold. No links in the body.** Same reasons.
- **One idea per line-group.** Read on a phone.
- **The question last**, where a cut ends in one.
- **3,000 char cap**, and the fold still decides whether anyone reads past line two.

Run every Stream B piece through the same `preflight` — it is format-agnostic and the
sanitisation gate is the whole point of it.

## What is relaxed

- **No verified incident required.** A thesis is allowed to be a thesis.
- **No "And yet" cap** — that is a Stream A signature and does not belong here.
- **Opinion may be asserted** without a mechanism behind it. Say "I think" and mean it.
- **Repetition is fine, and is the point.** The same claim from six angles is the model
  working, not laziness. Hormozi's audience hears the same twenty ideas continuously.

---

## What must NEVER be relaxed

This is the part that makes volume safe rather than expensive.

**1 · No invented results, metrics, testimonials or case studies.** Ever, in any lane.
Kira's landing page once carried invented testimonials; they were removed as a genuine
Australian Consumer Law exposure, not as a matter of taste. A fabricated result is not a
"marketing claim", it is a misleading representation, and at volume you will not remember
which ones were real.

**2 · No number about our own products that has not been established.** Opinion is free.
*"We ship a product every N weeks"* is not opinion.

**3 · No naming another company in a teardown without thinking about the relationship
first.** Analysing a named competitor publicly forecloses a partnership you might want —
and several of the interesting ones are potential distributors, not rivals. Write the
teardown; publish it with the name removed, or not at all.

**4 · Every price shown carries its tax qualifier.** "+ GST" or the buyer's local
equivalent. Portfolio rule, applies to a social post as much as a pricing page.

**5 · Stream B never claims Stream A's authority.** A thesis post must not be dressed as a
post-mortem. The series' value is that when Dennis says "this happened", it happened.

## Cadence, honestly

At four pillars a month and six cuts each you are at roughly **30 pieces a month** — a
long way short of a hundred a week, and about the ceiling for one operator writing in his
own voice without it becoming someone else's voice.

The way past that ceiling is more **platforms**, not more ideas: the same cut on LinkedIn,
X, and as a newsletter section is three placements of one piece of work. Which is, again,
exactly what the model actually is.

**The bottleneck is pillars, not cuts.** Six are listed above. Write one properly and the
week's posts fall out of it.
