Before You Buy One. №6

A hundred and forty jobs on our list, sorted by how they actually have to get done:

About half are mechanical. Something crosses a line, we check it against the system that holds the record, we write it from a template, we apply the owner's rules, it goes. No AI anywhere in it.

About a quarter are a conversation.

About one in seven genuinely needs judgement — and that seventh will take most of the work to build.

The rest sit in between: the system gets everything ready, a person decides.

Those last two lines are the useful ones. Effort and volume come apart badly here, and it explains something I kept noticing in demos and couldn't put my finger on. The clever seventh is the impressive part, so it is the part that gets shown — and it is a thin slice of what a business actually needs done in a week.

Here is how to use that in the room.

Write down the ten jobs you actually want taken off your desk. Not the exciting ones. The ten that eat Tuesday.

Hand the list over and ask them to sort it: which of these need judgement, and which are just rules being followed?

If everything on your list comes back as the clever kind, they have priced the demo rather than your week. If most of it comes back as rules, ask what the AI is doing in the price.

The unimpressive half is where your value is. It wants to be explainable far more than it wants to be clever.
