Before You Buy One. №3

Eight words you'll hear in any "agentic" pitch, what each one actually means, and what it changes about what you're buying.

Model. The AI itself — the part that reads and writes. You pay each time it runs, and it can give a different answer tomorrow from the same starting point. That's fine for judgement and wrong for anything that has to be identical every time.

Prompt. The instructions the model is given. Worth knowing because "we've prompted it not to" is a request, not a restriction. It describes what they've asked for, not what the thing can do.

Agent. A model that's been given tools — the ability to act, not just write. This is the word doing the most work in the whole pitch, and the one to slow down on.

Tools. What it's allowed to reach: your email, your accounting system, your calendar. Ask for the list. "It has access to your systems" is not a list.

Orchestration. Deciding which part does what, in what order. Ask whether that decision is itself made by AI, or by ordinary software. Both are legitimate; only one is predictable.

Guardrails. Whatever stops it doing something daft. Ask what kind of thing they are. AI checking AI can be talked round; a limit built into the software cannot.

Context window. The AI's short-term memory for one conversation. It does not survive being closed, so if the work lives there, it doesn't survive an approval that lands tomorrow.

Token. In this setting, the key that lets them into your systems, which usually keeps working long after the meeting. Ask how you cancel it.

None of this makes you an expert, and it isn't meant to. It makes you hard to bluff, which is a different and more useful thing.
