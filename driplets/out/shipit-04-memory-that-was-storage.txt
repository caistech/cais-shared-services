She remembered everything. She couldn't recall a word of it.

The memory loop was the part I was most confident about.

It's what Kira is — an owner explains how his business actually works, over months, and she holds it.

Built, tested, and published as a shared package for the other products to use.

Then someone came back for a second conversation and she didn't know him.

Not vaguely. She knew a conversation had happened — the flag saying "this person has history" was true and correct.

She just couldn't produce a single thing from it.

The recall query filtered on the agent's id matching the agent's id. Which sounds tautological, and is — except that the path we'd documented and recommended leaves that field empty.

And empty isn't equal to anything. Including itself.

So every fact she had chosen to keep was excluded from the one query that needed it.

Nothing errored. Nothing was wrong.

And yet she couldn't tell him one thing he'd told her.

The obvious fix is to check that memory is being written.

We had. It was — rows present, correct, growing steadily.

Storage was perfect and recall was zero. From the outside, those two are the same picture.

The probe we run now doesn't ask whether history exists. It asks her to say something from it, and fails if she can't.

What's your test that memory works, as opposed to storage?

I thought I had one.
