Before You Buy One. №7

A very reasonable question, put to me directly this week: surely you set up a team of specialists in advance — one that knows how you write quotes, one that knows your contacts, one that writes the letter — and the system hands work to whichever one fits.

It is the picture nearly everyone has, and you will see it on a slide. I have argued the other way, and I want to put the argument and my doubt about it in the same place, because you are going to have to judge somebody else's version of this.

The argument is that what you want ready in advance is not a set of assistants sitting there waiting. It is the instructions: what this one is for, what it is allowed to see, what it is allowed to propose — written down and agreed before any work turns up. That gives you everything "prepared" is meant to give, with nothing actually running.

And most of what people want a permanent assistant for turns out, on inspection, to be a permanent fact. Not something that reads the owner's last twenty quotes every single time, but the shape of how he writes a quote, worked out once and written down.

The doubt is that this is extremely convenient for someone whose setup has nowhere to put a permanently running assistant anyway. I cannot fully separate my reasoning from my own constraints, and I would rather say so than pretend.

So do not ask a vendor which approach is right. Ask why they chose theirs — and whether they would still choose it if their setup allowed the other.

Somebody who can answer that has thought about it. Somebody who tells you their way is simply how it is done has not, and you will be paying for that either way.
